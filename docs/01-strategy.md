# 01 — Strategy

## Reframe

SuqCheck is not a consumer price-comparison app that hopes ads appear. It is **pricing infrastructure**: a fixed Addis panel that resolves **4,800 SKU×shop cells every week**, sold first to brands who already lose margin to unauthorised retail markups.

Coverage is **ambassador-backed**. The crowd adds freshness, receipts, and shops beyond the 120 — upside, not a dependency. That is the opposite of enumerator-only systems that die when the grant ends (see Esoko in [02-comparables-evidence.md](02-comparables-evidence.md)).

---

## Macro and structural case

- Household staples in Addis are sold through a dense mix of minimarts and dukas; manufacturer recommended prices are routinely exceeded. Sagaci's African retail audit found only **~25% of retailers complied with MRP**, with **~75% priced above** (Sagaci Research, African pricing/compliance studies — cite the specific deck in the room).  
- Brands already buy periodic syndicated tracking or one-off field audits; neither gives **weekly** shop-level compliance on a named basket.  
- Ethiopia's smartphone and telebirr rails are thick enough for crowd incentives: Ethio telecom **90.12M** customers, **45.2%** smartphone penetration; telebirr **60.6M** customers (figures as of 23 July 2026 company disclosures).  
- FX for all conversions in this pack: **158 ETB/USD** ([ASSUMPTION]; NBE indicative 159.79 on 23 July 2026) — see [05-money.md](05-money.md).

The structural opening: **law already forces the evidence into the open**. SuqCheck photographs what shops must post.

---

## Legal evidence supply

**Primary national duty — Trade Competition and Consumers Protection Proclamation No. 813/2013, Article 15(1):** traders must post a price list in a conspicuous place **or** affix price tags. Proclamation 813 repealed **685/2010**; do **not** cite Article 23 of 685 — that is dead law.

**Addis enforcement layer — Directive 159/2024** (Addis Ababa City Administration, Village Trade Control Procedure): scoped to Addis; typically **2–3 controllers per village** working a control table that includes price-list display. This is why a single posted-list photo can replace 28 manual entries — and why shop access is a real risk (shops may read photography as inspection). Mitigations in [04-operating-model.md](04-operating-model.md) §2 and [06-implementation-90-days.md](06-implementation-90-days.md).

Cite **813/2013 Art. 15 first**, then **159/2024** as the local enforcement reality.

## Competitive map (honest)

| Player | What they are | Gap SuqCheck fills |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Sagaci Research** | African retail audit / syndicated tracking | Periodic, expensive, not a weekly Addis panel you can subscribe to as a feed |
| **Instagrocer / online grocers** | Commerce | Price is a merchandising input, not a multi-brand compliance product; no cross-shop panel |
| **Field agencies / Trax-class** | Store audit SaaS abroad | Trax ~$15k–40k/mo estimated, **not operating here**; local agencies price like Field Agent conversions (see [05](05-money.md)) |
| **Bizom** | Retail execution SaaS | Listed ~$3,000/mo (~474k ETB) — wrong shape for a 120-shop Addis staple panel |
| **Premise** | Global crowd observations | Cost-per-capture benchmark ($0.06–0.40); not an Ethiopia brand compliance product |
| **WFP mVAM / WB RTP / CSA** | Institutional price collection | Questionnaire economics ($20–40 F2F); six-week cycles; procurement, not brand SaaS |
| **Esoko (cautionary)** | Agri price info | Enumerator cost, weak B2B anchor — failure mode we design against ([02](02-comparables-evidence.md)) |

No credible local incumbent sells **weekly, shop-level, multi-brand staple compliance** on a fixed Addis panel. That is the wedge.

## Five-layer moat

1. **Legal surface** — mandated posted lists under 813/2013 + 159/2024 make the cheapest high-yield capture path available; competitors who ignore it re-pay for manual entry.  
2. **Route density** — 8 people × fixed 15-shop routes beats one-off agency missions on cost (90 ETB vs 1,550–3,900 ETB/store international bound — [05](05-money.md)).  
3. **Evidence engine** — every price is gated, weighted, and explainable; trust is a product feature brands can audit.  
4. **Basket + customer concentration** — Samanu-class manufacturers own large SKU shares; one design partner validates a quarter of the panel.  
5. **Shared fixed cost** — panel opex ~110k ETB/month whether one buyer or eight; brands 2–8 sit on **~95% incremental gross margin** ([05](05-money.md) §6). Data compounds; agency missions reset.

Crowd and points deepen moat layers 2–3 over time but are **not** required for week-1 coverage.
